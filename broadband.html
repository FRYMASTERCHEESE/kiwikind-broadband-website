Broadband types, made simple

🏠 Fibre (UFB)

If you have a fibre box (ONT) on the wall, start here. Usually best for home streaming, gaming and working from home if we can supply the address.

🌾 Rural or fixed wireless

Internet through a wireless network, typically using suitable router equipment. Check rural coverage and data limits if fibre is unavailable.

☎️ Copper (ADSL/VDSL)

Internet through an older phone line. A fallback where still offered; we check the address before confirming it.

Can I use my existing Spark, One NZ or other modem/router?

Sometimes, but not every modem is compatible. Fibre uses a fibre box (ONT) and a router with an Ethernet WAN/Internet port. A DSL-only phone-line modem cannot connect directly to fibre. Some provider routers are locked or cannot use the network settings KiwiKind needs.

A fibre router needs compatible Ethernet ports and supplier settings. Higher-speed connections may require upgraded hardware. Rural and fixed-wireless connections may need a compatible SIM router or supplier-approved equipment.

What do the fibre speeds mean?

Download speed matters for streaming and downloads; upload speed matters for video calls and sending files. KiwiKind will list exact download and upload speeds only after verifying the supplier's current catalogue. Availability depends on the service address and equipment.
